The best workflows are almost invisible. They create enough structure to keep work moving, then get out of the way.

## Begin with the constraint

Before choosing a tool, write down what the work must preserve. For this publication, the constraints are clear:

- reading must remain comfortable;
- publishing must remain ordinary;
- the source should be understandable;
- maintenance should not become a second hobby.

These constraints are more useful than a list of fashionable technologies. They turn choices into comparisons instead of impulses.

## Separate making from judging

Trying to create and simplify at the same moment often produces timid work. A better rhythm is:

1. establish the intended quality;
2. make the complete thing;
3. inspect it in context;
4. remove complexity without lowering the result.

The order matters. You cannot protect a quality bar that was never made visible.

### Leave a clear next step

At the end of a session, record one concrete next action. “Continue the design” is vague. “Adjust the mobile article header so metadata wraps before the title” gives tomorrow a useful beginning.

Good workflow is less about moving faster than about losing less attention between one decision and the next.
